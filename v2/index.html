Points-tested permanent residency with no employer or state tie. Live and work anywhere in Australia from the day you land.

Skilled Nominated

Why MARA registration matters

Anyone can call themselves a consultant. Only a registered agent can act for you.

The Office of the Migration Agents Registration Authority is the Australian Government's regulator. Registration is not a marketing badge; it is a legal standard we are held to.

01 · Legal standing

Authorised to give immigration assistance

It is an offence under the Migration Act for an unregistered person to give Australian immigration assistance. Your application is prepared and lodged by someone permitted to do it.

02 · Code of Conduct

Written fees, written advice, no guarantees

The Code requires a written agreement before we take a fils, honest advice on your prospects, and no promises of an outcome. That is how we work with every client.

03 · Accountability

A regulator you can complain to

If a registered agent falls short, there is a formal complaints process with real consequences. With an unregistered intermediary, there is nothing.

Verify any agent before you pay them. The public register lists every registered migration agent, their MARN, and their registration status.

Questions we are asked most

Straight answers.

MARA is the Australian Government's regulator for migration agents. Only registered agents can legally provide Australian immigration assistance and represent you in a visa application. Truvia's team works under a registered agent, so your case is handled to Australian regulatory standards rather than by an unlicensed intermediary.

Subclass 189, 190 and 491 are points-tested. You need at least 65 points to lodge an Expression of Interest, though invitations usually go to higher scores. Points come from age, English, skilled employment, qualifications, Australian study, partner skills and state nomination. We calculate your score precisely before you invest in the process.

Based on current processing, Subclass 189 takes roughly 7 to 9 months, Subclass 190 around 12 to 17 months and Subclass 491 around 12 to 18 months from lodgement. Budget preparation time before that for the skills assessment, English test and EOI.

Yes. Your qualifications and experience must be verified by the assessing authority for your occupation before you can lodge. VETASSESS, TRA, ACS, Engineers Australia, AHPRA and CPA each have their own standards. Choosing the right authority and occupation code is where most applications go wrong, and it is a core part of what we do.

Competent English (IELTS 6 in each band or equivalent) is the minimum for most skilled visas, but English is worth up to 20 points. Superior English (IELTS 8 or PTE 79) earns the maximum. For many applicants, improving the English score is the fastest way to become competitive, which is why we coach PTE, IELTS and OET in-house.
